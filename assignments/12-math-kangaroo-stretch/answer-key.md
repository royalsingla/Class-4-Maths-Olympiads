# Week 12+ (Optional) — Math Kangaroo-Style Stretch — Answer Key

Total: 60 points (5 × 3 + 5 × 4 + 5 × 5).

## Tier 1 — 3 points each

**Q1. B) △** — The pattern alternates ○, △, ○, △, ○, so the next shape is △.

**Q2. B) 5** — The big triangle is divided into 4 small triangles (3
"corner" triangles plus 1 "upside-down" triangle in the middle). Counting
those 4, plus the big triangle itself as a 5th, gives 5 triangles in
total. No other combination of the pieces forms a triangle.

**Q3. C) 4** — Worst case, you could pull out all 3 red balls first. The
4th ball you take is then guaranteed to be blue (since there are only 2
colours and you've exhausted the larger colour group). In general: the
answer is (the bigger group's count) + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4.

**Q4. C** — Option C correctly mirrors the figure: the pole stays right
next to the mirror line, the flag flips to point the opposite way, and the
dot stays at the same height on the other side. (A just copies the shape
without flipping it — the flag still points the same way, which a mirror
would never do. B flips it top-to-bottom instead of left-to-right. D spins
it 180°, which flips it both ways at once.)

**Q5. B) 2nd** — Spike = 7th. Jerry finished 3 places ahead of Spike: 7 −
3 = 4th. Tom finished 2 places ahead of Jerry: 4 − 2 = 2nd.

## Tier 2 — 4 points each

**Q6. B) 5** — The grid has 4 small unit squares, plus the big 2×2 square
itself, giving 5 squares in total.

**Q7. B) 10** — Let the smaller number be *x*; the larger is 4*x*. Then
*x* + 4*x* = 50 → 5*x* = 50 → *x* = 10.

**Q8. C) 75 g** — 1 orange = 2 kiwis = 2 × 50 g = 100 g. Since apple +
banana = orange + kiwi = 100 g + 50 g = 150 g, and apple = banana, each
one weighs 150 ÷ 2 = 75 g.

**Q9. B) 6** — To get from the dot to the star you need exactly 2 "right"
moves and 2 "down" moves, in any order. The number of distinct orders is
"4 choose 2" = 6: RRDD, RDRD, RDDR, DRRD, DRDR, DDRR.

**Q10. C) 11** — Working backwards: 45 ÷ 3 = 15 (this was "2× the number,
minus 7"); 15 + 7 = 22 (this was "2× the number"); 22 ÷ 2 = 11.

## Tier 3 — 5 points each

**Q11. A) Apple** — Bob = cherry and Dan = date are given directly. That
leaves apple and banana for Ann and Cara. Ann doesn't like banana, so Ann
must like apple — and that leaves banana for Cara, which is consistent
with "Cara doesn't like apple."

**Q12. A) 1/16** — Each quadrant is 1/4 of the big square. The shaded
triangle is 1 of 4 equal triangles inside that quadrant, so it's 1/4 of
1/4 = 1/16 of the whole big square.

**Q13. C) 9** — The 2-digit numbers whose digits add to 9 are: 18, 27,
36, 45, 54, 63, 72, 81, 90 — that's 9 numbers.

**Q14. D) S** — In a row of 4 squares that wraps around to form 4 sides
of a cube, the 1st and 3rd squares end up opposite each other, and so do
the 2nd and 4th. Here the row is Q, X, R, S — so X (2nd) is opposite S
(4th). (P and T, attached above and below X, become the cube's top and
bottom faces, and end up opposite each other instead.)

**Q15. B) Day 8** — Track the snail's height at the *top* of each day's
climb (before any night-time slide): Day 1 → 3 m (slides back to 1 m), Day
2 → 4 m (back to 2 m), Day 3 → 5 m (back to 3 m), Day 4 → 6 m (back to 4
m), Day 5 → 7 m (back to 5 m), Day 6 → 8 m (back to 6 m), Day 7 → 9 m
(back to 7 m), Day 8 → 7 + 3 = 10 m — exactly the top, so it climbs out
and stops. This is a classic puzzle precisely because the "naive" answer
(10 days, from 10 ÷ 1 m net gain per day) is wrong — the snail is already
out before the final slide-back would even happen.
