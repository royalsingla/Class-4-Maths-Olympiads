# Week 3 — Factors, Multiples & Number Patterns — Answer Key

**Q1. C) 6** — 18 = 6 × 3, so 6 is a factor. (4, 5, 8 do not divide 18 exactly.)

**Q2. B) 35** — 35 = 7 × 5. (30, 40, 45 are not multiples of 7.)

**Q3. B) 6** — Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. Greatest common factor = 6.

**Q4. B) 12** — Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16... Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18... First common one = 12.

**Q5. C) 29** — 29 has no factors other than 1 and itself. (21 = 3×7, 27 = 3×3×3, 33 = 3×11.)

**Q6. C) 6** — Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 — that's 6 factors.

**Q7. C) 15** — 15 = 3 × 5 is the smallest number that both 3 and 5 divide exactly.

**Q8. C) 48** — 9 × 5 = 45, 9 × 6 = 54, so 48 falls between multiples of 9 and isn't one itself. (27 = 9×3, 36 = 9×4, 63 = 9×7.)

**Q9. C) 48** — Each number is double the one before it (×2): 3, 6, 12, 24, 48.

**Q10. B) 70** — Each number decreases by 10: 100, 90, 80, 70, 60.

**Q11. C) 36** — These are square numbers: 1², 2², 3², 4², 5², 6² = 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36.

**Q12. B) 9** — These are odd numbers increasing by 2: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.

**Q13. C) 32** — Each number is double the one before it: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.

**Q14. B) 7 groups, 0 left** — 42 ÷ 6 = 7 exactly, so 7 full groups with nothing left over.

**Q15. B) 8** — 8 is a factor of 24 (24 ÷ 8 = 3) and also a multiple of 4 (4 × 2 = 8). (6 is a factor of 24 but not a multiple of 4; 10 and 16 are not even factors of 24.)
